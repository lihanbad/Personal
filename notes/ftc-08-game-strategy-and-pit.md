## Match Strategy = Points, Not Vibes

Before coding a single thing, **read the game manual** and answer:

1. What scores the **most points per second**?
2. What scores the **most reliable** points (lowest risk of dropping pieces)?
3. What can win **alliance selection** (being easy to work with, robust)?

### Auto First, Teleop Second
A solid 60–80 point auto wins matches when teleops are equal. Prioritize the
**safe, repeatable** auto over the fancy one.

### Alliance Talk Before the Match
- 60 seconds to plan: "I do X, you do Y, we share zone Z."
- Confirm **who takes which side** and who does the **endgame**.
- Never promise a mechanic that's broken today.

## The Real "Strategy" Checklist

- [x] Know both teams' robots (afk levels, autos, cycle speeds).
- [x] Decide the auto plan and who scores where.
- [x] Assign roles: driver, operator, human player, backup driver.
- [x] Agree on a **default** if a robot dies mid-match.

## Pit Operations

### Pre-Match Check
- Battery charged, terminal screws tight, voltage readout sane.
- Motor plugs seated, servo horns tight, wires strain-relieved.
- Wheels/gears spin free; **nothing rattling** loose.
- Gyro cal, camera pointing straight, robot "start config" valid.

### Between Matches
```
CHARGE BATTERY  ->  SWAP/INSPECT GAME PIECE MECHANISM  ->  RE-CAL IMU IF NEEDED
```

### Match-Day Tips
- One driver change max; **practice with the simulator/field**.
- Log telemetry after every match; fix the *biggest* risk first.
- If something breaks, fix it with the **safest quick fix** — don't redesign mid-event.

## Awards vs Matches

FTC gives **equal weight** to robot performance and judged awards. Even mid-pack
teams can experience a **Think Award** season by:

- Keeping an organized **engineering notebook** (ideation → CAD → testing).
- Documenting **failures** as aggressively as successes.
- Real community outreach (schools, STEM events) with photos/numbers.
