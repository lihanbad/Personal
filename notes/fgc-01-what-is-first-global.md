## What is FGC?

FIRST Global is a UN-partnered international robotics competition. **One or two
teams per country** (typically ages 14–18) represent their nation. Instead of
hundreds of teams in one place, FGC is a **global gathering** — up to ~190
countries compete, often generating national TV coverage.

## The FGC Difference vs FTC/FRC

| Feature            | FGC                                            |
|--------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Team size          | Small national team (a few students).          |
| Robot kit          | **Identical Kit of Parts for every country.**  |
| Build responsibility| Students build the robot — mentors give advice, not hands. |
| Mentoring limit    | Stringent — only 2 "Global Mentors" (judges/volunteers) can advise the build. |
| Language of rules  | Rules written to be **culture/tech-neutral**.  |
| Awards            | Medallions (gold/silver/bronze) + special awards. |

## Format

- Matches are played in **alliances** of robots on a game field.
- Sessions: **autonomous** + **teleoperated** phases.
- The robot is designed to be built/run **without highly specific tools** —
  everything is in the Kit of Parts.
- Winners qualify for **Team of the Alliance** finals each year.

## Philosophy: "Culture of Collaboration"

FGC's tagline is about **uniting the world through STEM**. That means:

- Countries trade spare parts and knowledge at pit row.
- Robots are judged for **innovation & sustainability**, not just speed.
- Emphasizes **recycling / sustainable materials** in the robot kit.

## The Public Profile

- Host city rotates yearly (Mexico City, Dubai, Geneva, Singapore, Athens, Doha...).
- Often broadcast like an international sporting event with flags and anthems.
- This is the "safest" robotics competition culture-wise — but that doesn't mean
  the build is easy: your robot must travel the world and work on day one.
